# legal-appeal

## Run both directions

Whichever side wins at first instance, ask the other question too: if the user wins, how could the opponent appeal? If the user loses, what appeal points genuinely exist? A one-sided appellate analysis misses the case-killer that could come back the other way.

## What to check

Error of law, the applicable factual-appellate standard (appellate courts generally do not re-try facts the way a trial court found them - the actual standard is jurisdiction- and court-specific, check the pack rather than assuming), procedural unfairness, adequacy of reasons, jurisdiction to hear the appeal at all, discretion exercised wrongly, admissibility of new evidence on appeal, and whether the point was actually preserved below (an unpreserved point is often not available on appeal at all).

## Precedent distinguishing, done properly

A case is not "distinguishable" without naming the legally material difference - compare statutory text, material facts, procedural posture, policy, jurisdiction, and court level. "The facts are a bit different" is not an analysis.
