## A. Threshold and procedural grounds: the Respondent's strongest position

### A.1 Limitation/jurisdiction (ISS-001): the single strongest point available, but not an unqualified one

The burden of proving an ET1 was presented in time sits with the Claimant, not the Respondent (ERA 1996 s.111(2)(a), independently verified live against legislation.gov.uk per `procedure/limitation.md`). On the Claimant's own fact ledger:

- The effective date of termination is `ASSUMED`, not documented (F-002).
- The ACAS EC certificate date ("Day B") is likewise `ASSUMED` (F-003).
- The ACAS EC contact date ("Day A"), without which the ERA 1996 s.207B stop-the-clock calculation cannot be run at all, is `UNKNOWN` and not stated anywhere (F-004).
- The actual date the ET1 was, or will be, presented is not stated anywhere in the facts supplied.

**The Respondent's position**: put the Claimant to strict proof of all four dates before conceding jurisdiction on any head. This is not a technicality invented for tactical advantage; `procedure/limitation.md` and `issues.json` (produced independently of this opposition analysis) already reach the same conclusion from the other side of the ledger, and the matter's own moot record (`hearing-5.json`) dismissed the claim on this exact basis after four prior hearings gave the Claimant the opportunity to produce these documents and none was produced.

**Two qualifications the Respondent must not overstate, honestly stated rather than glossed over:**

1. **The ERA and Equality Act limitation tests are not the same test**, and a Respondent's submission that "limitation disposes of everything" would misstate the law. ERA 1996 s.111(2)(b) allows extension only where presentation within three months was "not reasonably practicable": a narrow discretion. Equality Act 2010 s.123 allows extension wherever the Tribunal considers it "just and equitable": a materially wider discretion, and it applies independently to the discrimination-based heads (ISS-005 to ISS-008) even if the ERA heads are found out of time. If those heads are ever properly pleaded (currently they are not; see Part B.4 below), a limitation defence to the ERA heads would not automatically defeat them.
2. **On the facts as currently stated, today's date falls inside the primary three-month period even on the Claimant's own worst-case arithmetic** (2 months 3 weeks < 3 months); `procedure/limitation.md` and `chronology.json` CHR-003 both say so, and this opposition analysis does not dispute that arithmetic. The Respondent's real limitation argument is not "the claim is presently shown to be out of time" (it is not, on the assumed EDT); it is "the Claimant has not yet discharged the burden of proving it is in time," which is a different and narrower proposition, and the honest one available. A Respondent that overclaims the first version invites exactly the correction a real Tribunal would give it.

## B. Merits grounds: presently unsupported on the Claimant's own ledger, with real (not manufactured) legal points in reserve

Per `issues.json`, every element of every substantive head (ISS-002 to ISS-009) is currently tagged `UNSUPPORTED` or, at best, `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`. The Respondent's position is not that these claims have been disproved (no Respondent-side evidence exists in this record either, and this opposition case does not pretend otherwise), but that the Claimant has not discharged the evidential burden that sits on the Claimant for several specific elements, and that, once the missing documents surface, genuine legal tests apply that could cut either way.

### B.1 Automatic unfair dismissal and whistleblowing detriment (ISS-003, ISS-004): the Claimant's own lead theory, tested properly

The Claimant's case theory (`final/case_theory_memo.md` Section 3) correctly identifies this as its strongest head and correctly names ***Kuzel v Roche Products Ltd*** [2008] EWCA Civ 380 (burden never shifts to the Claimant to prove the dismissal reason, but the Claimant must first adduce "some evidence" the disclosure was the reason) and ***Beatt v Croydon Health Services NHS Trust*** [2017] EWCA Civ 401 (whether a disclosure was protected is for the Tribunal to assess objectively). Both are already logged `VERIFIED_SECONDARY_SOURCE`/`BINDING` in `authorities.json` (AUTH-003, AUTH-004) and this opposition analysis does not dispute their correctness: disputing binding Court of Appeal authority because it happens to assist the other side would be exactly the unsustainable advocacy `agents/opposition/ROLE.md` forbids.

The Respondent's genuine points of resistance sit not in the doctrine but in two gaps the Claimant's own ledger discloses, plus one substantive legal test the Claimant's theory does not yet engage with:

**(i) The "public interest" requirement (ERA 1996 s.43B(1), as amended) is a real, live obstacle on facts this thin, not a formality.** Section 43B(1) requires the worker to hold a reasonable belief that the disclosure is made "in the public interest", a requirement inserted by the Enterprise and Regulatory Reform Act 2013 s.17 specifically to reverse *Parkins v Sodexho Ltd* [2002] IRLR 109, which had allowed a complaint about the discloser's own contract of employment alone to qualify. Verified this session (independent web search, cross-checked across Lexology, No5 Chambers, and Thomas Mansfield Employment Law commentary, consistent across all three, `VERIFIED_SECONDARY_SOURCE`): s.17 ERRA 2013 was enacted precisely so that a complaint of a purely personal nature, with no wider public interest implications, no longer qualifies. ***Chesterton Global Ltd v Nurmohamed*** [2017] EWCA Civ 979 (Court of Appeal; verified this session via Croner-i, CMS Law-Now, Devereux Chambers, and Employment Cases Update reporting, consistent across all four, `VERIFIED_SECONDARY_SOURCE`) confirms the test is whether the worker's belief that the disclosure was in the public interest was itself objectively reasonable, and that a disclosure can still qualify even where it also serves the discloser's own interest, provided a section of the public beyond the discloser is also affected. **Applied here**: `facts.json` F-010 confirms the content, recipient, form, and date of the alleged disclosure(s) are not stated at all. If, once produced, the disclosure turns out to be solely about the Claimant's own individual grievance, treatment, or contract (with no other worker, patient, or member of the public shown to be affected), it does not meet s.43B(1) as amended, however sincerely believed, and neither ISS-003 nor ISS-004 can succeed regardless of what Kuzel or Beatt say about burden and objectivity, because there would be no qualifying disclosure to apply either test to. This is not presently established either way on the facts given; it is flagged as a real question the missing F-010 content must answer, not assumed against the Claimant.

**(ii) The disciplinary process may be an independent, unconnected reason, and the Claimant bears the burden of showing otherwise.** F-011 confirms a disciplinary process preceded termination; F-012 confirms the sequencing of that process relative to the alleged disclosure(s) is not stated. Kuzel requires the Claimant to produce "some evidence" the disclosure was a reason (or the reason) for the treatment before the burden-testing exercise engages at all. On the facts as given, it is equally consistent with the ledger that the disciplinary process began, and dismissal followed, for reasons genuinely unconnected to any disclosure; the Respondent is entitled to hold the Claimant to that burden rather than conceding causation because a disclosure and a dismissal both occurred within some period of each other. This is a real evidential point, not a rhetorical one: `chronology_gaps.md` (produced independently of this opposition analysis) already records that no ordering between these events can presently be established from the facts supplied.

**(iii) If the "contradictory internal emails" (F-013) prove to be a co-worker's act rather than a management decision, the Respondent has a genuine statutory defence available for the detriment head specifically.** ERA 1996 s.47B(1A)-(1E) (inserted by Enterprise and Regulatory Reform Act 2013 s.19, effective 25 June 2013; already independently verified live against legislation.gov.uk in this matter's own record, `authorities.json` AUTH-002 note) gives a worker a direct right of action against a co-worker who subjects them to a s.47B detriment and treats that act as also done by the employer, **but gives the employer an "all reasonable steps" defence** to that vicarious liability. If the emails at F-013 turn out to be an individual manager's or colleague's conduct outside any authorised disciplinary decision, and the Respondent can show it took all reasonable steps to prevent such conduct, this is a genuine avenue of defence to the detriment head specifically, distinct from, and not dependent on, the causation and public-interest points above. This cannot be assessed further until the content, authorship, and status of the emails (F-014) is known, and this opposition case does not assume the defence is made out: only that it exists and would be available on the right facts.

**Respondent's honest position on ISS-003/ISS-004**: consistent with `issues.json`'s own tagging and the moot record's final disposition, this head is currently unproven on both sides: not because the Respondent has shown an innocent explanation, but because the Claimant has not yet shown the elements Kuzel and Beatt require to be tested. The public-interest point (i) is a genuine, additional legal hurdle the Claimant's own case theory does not yet address at all, and is the single most concrete substantive point available to the Respondent on this head.

### B.2 Ordinary unfair dismissal (ISS-002, remaining elements): doctrine held in reserve, not yet engaged

Beyond the continuity gate (Part A.2), ISS-002's remaining elements require the Respondent to show a potentially fair reason (ERA 1996 s.98(1)-(2)) and that dismissal was fair in all the circumstances (s.98(4)). F-020 confirms the stated reason for dismissal is not disclosed anywhere in this record, so this opposition case cannot and does not assert what that reason was or that it was fair: doing so would be inventing a fact to make the Respondent's case look stronger than the record supports, which `agents/opposition/ROLE.md` forbids as directly as it forbids strawmanning the other side.

What can honestly be said: **once the actual reason and disciplinary file are produced**, the applicable legal framework is well settled and genuinely favours a Respondent that can show a properly conducted process: ***British Home Stores Ltd v Burchell*** [1978] IRLR 379 (EAT; verified this session via Croner-i, Personnel Today, Fieldfisher, and Wikipedia's case summary, consistent across all four, `VERIFIED_SECONDARY_SOURCE`) requires only a genuine belief in the misconduct, reasonable grounds for that belief, and a reasonable (not exhaustive) investigation, not proof to a criminal standard. ***Iceland Frozen Foods Ltd v Jones*** [1982] IRLR 439, [1983] ICR 17 (EAT, Browne-Wilkinson J, judgment 29 July 1982; verified this session via Croner-i, Wikipedia, and doihaveacase.co.uk, consistent across all three, `VERIFIED_SECONDARY_SOURCE`) confirms a Tribunal must not substitute its own view of the right course of action for the employer's, but ask only whether the decision to dismiss fell within a "band of reasonable responses" a reasonable employer might have adopted. These are named here as the doctrine that will govern the fairness question if and when the Respondent's stated reason is disclosed, not as a present defence, because there is presently nothing pleaded for either test to bite on.

### B.3 Procedural fairness and remedy (ISS-009): Polkey and the ACAS Code uplift, correctly logged this time

The Claimant's case theory and the moot record both note that "Polkey" was repeatedly relied on across Hearings 2 through 5 of this matter's own moot record without ever being logged in `authorities.json` with a verification trail: flagged at every one of those hearings as an uncured defect. As the opposition agent actually producing this analysis, that authority is verified here rather than repeated as an unlogged assertion: ***Polkey v A E Dayton Services Ltd*** [1988] AC 344 (House of Lords; verified this session via Wikipedia, Employment Law UK, and Personnel Today reporting, consistent across all three, `VERIFIED_SECONDARY_SOURCE`) holds that a dismissal can be unfair for procedural failings even where the employee might still have been fairly dismissed had a correct procedure been followed; but that the compensatory award may then be reduced, including to nil, by the tribunal's assessed percentage chance that dismissal would have occurred anyway. **The Respondent's position**: if a liability head is found against it, the existence of an actual disciplinary process (F-011), even one later found procedurally flawed, is the Respondent's strongest available Polkey argument, because it shows dismissal was not simply arbitrary; the size of any Polkey reduction depends entirely on the disciplinary file's content, which is not yet in this record. TULRCA 1992 s.207A's up-to-25% ACAS Code uplift, cutting the other way, is equally unassessable until that same file exists. This is genuinely a two-edged issue, not a one-sided Respondent advantage, and is presented as such.

### B.4 Disability discrimination cluster (ISS-005 to ISS-007) and victimisation (ISS-008): currently unpleadable, but not for a reason unique to the Respondent's benefit

This is the clearest area of overlap with the Claimant's own case theory (`final/case_theory_memo.md` Section 4) and the moot record's Hearing 1 strike, and the opposition case does not manufacture disagreement with either for the sake of appearing more robust than the record supports:

- **ISS-005 (disability status, gateway)**: F-021 confirms no impairment is named: not a diagnosis, not a lay description. **The Respondent's position**: hold the Claimant to proof of s.6 Equality Act 2010 status before engaging with ISS-006 or ISS-007 at all. This is not merely a pleading-gap argument, however; it is also a substantive one that could genuinely assist the Respondent once OH material is produced: ***J v DLA Piper UK LLP*** [2010] UKEAT/0263/09 (verified this session directly against BAILII's own case reference at `bailii.org/uk/cases/UKEAT/2010/0263_09_1506.html`, identified via search and cross-checked against Croner-i, Mental Health Law Online, and swarb.co.uk reporting; the BAILII page itself returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch this session, consistent with the access restriction already noted throughout this matter's record for AUTH-002 through AUTH-006, so this remains `VERIFIED_SECONDARY_SOURCE` rather than a first-party read) draws a real distinction between a qualifying clinical impairment and a reactive response to stressful circumstances (there, workplace stress) which does not by itself amount to a s.6 impairment, particularly where the "long-term" (12-month, or likely-to-last-12-month) element is not made out. **Applied here**: if the sickness absence at F-017/F-018 turns out to be a short-term reaction to the disciplinary process itself, rather than a free-standing, longer-term clinical condition, the Respondent has a genuine, non-fabricated basis to dispute disability status even once OH material is disclosed, not merely to note that it has not yet been disclosed. This cuts against the Respondent too, honestly: J v DLA Piper's own disposition was to send the disability question back for reconsideration rather than to resolve it against the claimant, and the EAT there flagged that "perceived disability" reasoning may independently apply where an employer acts because it believes someone to be disabled, whether or not that belief is later proved correct, a route the Claimant has not raised on this record but could.
- **ISS-006/ISS-007 (discrimination arising from disability; reasonable adjustments)**: gated by ISS-005, and additionally F-024 confirms no PCP or specific adjustment is identified at all. ***Environment Agency v Rowan*** [2008] ICR 218 (already logged `VERIFIED_SECONDARY_SOURCE`/`PERSUASIVE` in `authorities.json` AUTH-006, and independently checked for consistency this session) is fatal to ISS-007 in its current, unparticularised form for exactly the reason the Claimant's own case theory already concedes: a reasonable-adjustments claim requires the PCP, the comparator, and the disadvantage to be identified before reasonableness can be assessed at all. **The Respondent must concede honestly, not merely tactically**, that ***Gallop v Newport City Council*** [2013] EWCA Civ 1583 (already logged `VERIFIED_SECONDARY_SOURCE`/`BINDING` in `authorities.json` AUTH-005) is a real risk to the Respondent's knowledge defence if and when a disability is established: an employer cannot simply rubber-stamp an OH adviser's conclusion, or lack of one, and must exercise its own judgement on the primary facts. If OH material (F-015/F-016) turns out to show the Respondent was told of a qualifying condition and did nothing, Gallop is squarely against the Respondent, not for it; this opposition case names that risk rather than omitting it because it is unfavourable.
- **ISS-008 (victimisation)**: F-023 confirms no protected act under Equality Act 2010 s.27(2) is identified anywhere. **The Respondent's position**: this head cannot presently be met with anything beyond "not identified, therefore not yet a claim"; there is no additional substantive point available to the Respondent beyond the pleading gap itself, and this opposition case does not invent one.

## C. Honest assessment of this opposing case's actual strength

Per `agents/opposition/ROLE.md`, this must be stated plainly rather than softened in either direction, and it substantially mirrors this matter's own moot record (`hearing-5.json`), which this opposition analysis was produced independently of and arrives at largely the same place by a different route:

**The Respondent's strongest position is real, but it is currently an artefact of how little the Claimant's own fact ledger discloses, not an affirmative finding that the Respondent acted lawfully, fairly, or for a good reason.** No fact in this record says the Respondent's process was fair, its stated reason genuine, or its treatment of OH material adequate; those questions are simply unanswered, on both sides, not answered in the Respondent's favour. The correctly stated Respondent position is therefore threshold-and-evidential (hold the Claimant to strict proof of limitation, continuity, disability status, PCP, and protected act; hold the Claimant to the s.43B(1) public-interest test specifically, which the Claimant's own theory does not yet engage with at all) rather than a substantive defence of the Respondent's conduct, because nothing in this record, including from the Respondent's own side, establishes what that conduct actually was.

Three points cut specifically against overstating the Respondent's position, named here because a genuinely candid opposing case must name them rather than let the threshold points stand unqualified:

1. **The limitation point, on the facts as they presently stand, does not show the claim is out of time: only that the Claimant has not yet proved it is in time.** Today's date falls inside the primary three-month window even on the Claimant's own worst-case arithmetic (Part A.1). A Respondent submission that treats limitation as already fatal would be arguing beyond what this record supports.
2. **The public-interest point (Part B.1(i)) and the co-worker vicarious-liability defence (Part B.1(iii)) are conditional, not established**; they depend entirely on the actual content of the disclosure and the actual authorship of the contradictory emails, neither of which is in this record. They are real legal tests that would apply on the right facts, not present factual findings.
3. **Gallop (Part B.4) is a genuine risk to the Respondent, not a point in its favour**, and is named as such rather than omitted because naming only favourable authority would misstate the actual state of the law applicable to this matter.

If the Claimant were to particularise F-010 (disclosure content/date/recipient), F-012 (sequencing), F-020 (stated reason), F-021/F-024 (impairment/PCP), and F-023 (protected act) with facts favourable to the Claimant, several of the Respondent's threshold points could weaken quickly, and the merits would then have to be met on their actual substance, including the genuine Respondent-adverse risk under Gallop, rather than on the Claimant's present pleading deficiency. This opposing case is strongest as a case-management and evidential-burden position at this stage of the matter; it is not, and should not be presented as, a demonstration that the Respondent's dismissal, disciplinary process, or handling of disability-related material was lawful.
