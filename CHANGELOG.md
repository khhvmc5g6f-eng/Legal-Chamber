### Added - Australia's first Authority Graph entries (Contract, Tort, Property, Criminal Law)

Continuing the user's instruction to extend the England & Wales/Scotland build-out to the remaining jurisdictions, four foundational entries were added to `jurisdictions/australia/authorities/` - this repository's first-ever populated Authority Graph content for Australia, previously `STRUCTURAL_DRAFT` with zero case-law content.

- `contract-unconscionable-conduct-normative-standard.json` - the normative standard for statutory unconscionable conduct (Australian Consumer Law s.21; ACCC v Lux Distributors Pty Ltd [2013] FCAFC 90).
- `tort-negligence-breach-four-factor-test.json` - the statutory four-factor breach-of-duty calculus (Civil Liability Act 2002 (NSW) s.5B; Tapp v Australian Bushmen's Campdraft & Rodeo Association Ltd [2022] HCA 11).
- `property-native-title-continuity-requirement.json` - native title's "substantially uninterrupted" continuity requirement (Native Title Act 1993 (Cth) s.223; Members of the Yorta Yorta Aboriginal Community v Victoria [2002] HCA 58).
- `criminal-conspiracy-intention-not-recklessness.json` - the intention element of Commonwealth conspiracy (Criminal Code (Cth) s.11.5; The Queen v LK; The Queen v RK (2010) 241 CLR 177).

The most significant finding from this pass is methodological: **Australia's primary-source access pattern was markedly harder than any jurisdiction covered so far.** AustLII, jade.io, and the High Court's own eresources/download routes returned HTTP 403 or unusable timeouts across dozens of URL attempts spanning all four research passes - far more consistently blocked than England & Wales's or Scotland's sources. Three of the four case nodes (Tort, Property, Criminal) are consequently recorded at `VERIFIED_SECONDARY_SOURCE`, corroborated instead via independent, professionally-authored secondary sources (a QUT academic case note, an NSW Judicial Commission bench book, a barrister's CPD paper) that themselves pin-cite the primary judgment's paragraphs - never via a third-party reader-proxy, consistent with this repository's access-terms rule. Each file states this honestly rather than presenting a secondary-sourced quote as a primary read. One research pass (the Property Law/native title entry) also caught and discarded an unreliable WebSearch-synthesized claim naming a High Court Justice who had not yet been appointed at the time of the judgment in question - a real, demonstrated instance of exactly the fabrication risk this repository's verification discipline exists to catch.

`docs/PRACTICE_AREAS.md`'s Contract/Tort/Property/Criminal Law rows now carry Australia alongside England & Wales and Scotland; `jurisdictions/australia/README.md` gained its first "Populated Authority Graph" section; `docs/HONEST_STATUS.md` and the top-level `README.md`'s jurisdictions table updated to show four (not three) jurisdictions with real Authority Graph content. Full regression: all 12 script selftests pass, all 4 Australia authority files pass schema validation and citation/style/year checks with zero findings.

### Operational note

Running 6 parallel research agents against CourtListener simultaneously caused a genuine livelock: a shared per-minute rate limit reset on every agent's retry, so none of them could make sustained progress while running concurrently. All 6 were stopped; the remaining topics (preliminary injunctions, summary judgment, qualified immunity) were completed sequentially in a single context instead, which resolved the per-minute contention but then hit CourtListener's separate hourly quota (50/hour) - work paused at that point and resumed once it reset. This is itself a useful, if unplanned, finding about how far real primary-source verification can scale in one session against a rate-limited public API - noted rather than hidden.

### Audit - three independent adversarial checks against the above, real findings fixed

Three fresh-context agents audited the new authority files, the citation-linter changes, and the whole session's diff for stale claims/broken cross-references, before this work was considered done. All three found real issues:

- **A significant functional gap**: `citation_lint.py`'s `us_reporter` pattern (both the original and the new reporter-recognition addition) matched zero pincited citations (`410 U.S. 113, 115 (1973)`) - extremely common in real legal writing, and exactly the shape a fabricated citation would most plausibly appear in. Fixed with an optional pincite group on both the shape pattern and the extraction regex, with new selftest coverage for both a real and a fabricated pincited reporter.
- **Fabricated date precision, the most serious finding**: nine of the twenty-five date fields across the twelve new England & Wales authority files asserted a specific day/month that the underlying verifying research agent's report never actually stated - added during transcription from general knowledge, unflagged. Confirmed against the original agent reports and corrected to year-only with an explicit `date_note` on each, matching the honest-uncertainty pattern already used correctly on six other entries in the same corpus. One entry (Filby v Mortgage Express (No 2) Ltd) had a deeper problem: `treatment: DISTINGUISHED` was wrong - a clean re-verification found the citing court discusses Mothew's reasoning but explicitly declines to apply or distinguish it, deciding the case on unrelated grounds. Corrected to `UNKNOWN` with the actual paragraph-level reasoning recorded, and the case name fixed (`(No 2) Ltd` had been dropped), with a now-real `primary_source_link`.
- **Stale documentation**: `citation/README.md` still described 3 shape patterns after 6 existed; `docs/HONEST_STATUS.md` and `LEGAL_CHAMBERS_STRESS_TEST_REPORT.md` both still asserted "no populated Authority Graph" and "no programmatic API" as current gaps, contradicting other parts of the same files that correctly said otherwise (predates this session, caught while auditing it) - both fixed with version-scoped language rather than silently deleting the historical record. `jurisdictions/england-wales/README.md`'s own count of how many files had their verification method corrected was itself off by one (said "two," was actually three) - fixed.

`.autistic/memory.json` added, recording the confirmed invariants/contradictions from this run for a future audit pass to build on rather than re-derive.

### Audit - Legal LLM Council (8-member parallel Claude review, no external LLM API keys configured), findings fixed

Real bugs, wiring gaps, and stale documentation, found and fixed the same session as the request:

- **Path traversal in `scripts/legal_cli.py`**: `matter_dir()` joined an unvalidated `matter_id` onto `matters/` with no sanitisation, so `matter_id="../evil-outside-matters"` escaped the gitignored `matters/` boundary entirely. Fixed with a plain-slug regex plus `os.path.realpath`/`os.path.commonpath` defense-in-depth; `scripts/legal_api.py` inherits the fix automatically since it delegates to the same function.
- **Silent success in `scripts/verify_matter_refs.py`**: a nonexistent matter directory returned "0 references checked, no broken cross-references found" (exit 0) instead of failing, the worst possible direction for a reference-integrity gate. Fixed with an explicit existence check.
- **`scripts/style_fix.py`'s quote-protection guarantee was broken in two ways**: quoted text spanning a line break had its wording silently edited (the exact failure the tool's own docstring says must never happen), and single-quoted text (the UK convention) wasn't recognised as protected at all. Fixed both, with word-boundary guards so the single-quote fix doesn't false-trigger on contractions like "don't."
- **`scripts/verify_court_name.py`'s metacharacter check was too broad**: it treated any period or parenthesis as a regex signal, silently dropping genuine literal court abbreviations from lookup, including the tool's own documented usage example ("S.D.N.Y."). Validated the fix against the full vendored 2,809-record `courts.json` table before finalising it.
- **Four `citation_lint.py` bugs**: `uk_law_report`'s abbreviation slot matched any capitalised word, false-flagging dates like `[2023] 12 May 2024`, tightened to a real allowlist. `canada_neutral` similarly false-flagged `2023 USD 500`, tightened the same way. `uk_statute` truncated hyphenated/ampersand Act names ("Anti-social Behaviour Act 2003" only matched "Behaviour Act 2003"). `us_reporter` was fully case-sensitive and invisible to lowercase citations ("593 u.s. 374 (2021)").
- **Twelve broken cross-references** ("Part XVII," "Part CLX," etc.) across 8 files, all citing a numbered Part of `docs/OPERATING_RULES.md` that only exists in the (deliberately) longer `docs/SPEC_FULL_TEXT.md` - including 4 instances this session's own earlier `0.1.7` work had introduced by copying an already-wrong citation without checking it.
