# HDC

The sixth regulator profile in this repository, following `ico.md`'s format - see `README.md`. This profile exists specifically to get the division of labour with `mcnz.md` and `nursing-council-nz.md` right, since conflating HDC's role with a professional council's fitness-to-practise role is a common real-world point of confusion (per this file's own task brief) and the regulator's own website does not spell the distinction out in one place.

## Statutory basis

The Health and Disability Commissioner is established under the **Health and Disability Commissioner Act 1994**, in force since October 1994. Its statutory purpose, confirmed live via `hdc.org.nz/about-us/purpose-vision/` (checked 2026-08-22), is to **"promote and protect the rights of consumers"** of health and disability services as set out in the **Code of Health and Disability Services Consumers' Rights** (the Code), a regulation under the Act in force since 1 July 1996, and to facilitate "the fair, simple, speedy, and efficient resolution of complaints." The Code sets out ten consumer rights (respect, freedom from discrimination, dignity and independence, services of an appropriate standard, informed consent, and the right to complain, among others) and applies to anyone providing or holding themselves out as providing a health service to the public - a materially broader remit than any single profession's regulator.

## What HDC is, that the professional councils are not

HDC is a **consumer-rights and complaint-resolution body**, not a fitness-to-practise regulator. It does not hold a register of practitioners, does not decide whether someone is fit to keep practising medicine or nursing, and (per its own published FAQ material checked live this session) does not itself impose professional sanctions. Its focus is whether a specific consumer's rights under the Code were breached in a specific instance of care, and what should happen about that - which can include recommendations to a provider, but is a different question from a council's ongoing question of "should this practitioner's registration, scope of practice, or conditions change."

## How HDC and the professional councils divide the work - verified live this session

This is the load-bearing part of this profile, cross-checked against `mcnz.md` and `nursing-council-nz.md`:

- **HDC is legislatively the front door.** Per the Medical Council's own published complaints material (see `mcnz.md`), all complaints about a doctor received by the Council must be referred to HDC. The Nursing Council's Registrar likewise takes HDC opinions into account when assessing a concern (see `nursing-council-nz.md`). HDC's own site describes itself as acting "as the initial recipient of complaints about health and disability services."
- **HDC can refer onward.** Per `hdc.org.nz/making-a-complaint/complaint-process/` (fetched live this session), one of the Commissioner's stated options on receiving a complaint is to "send your complaint to another organisation that is better placed to look into your concerns, such as" a professional registration authority like the Medical Council - i.e. the reverse referral described from the Council's side in `mcnz.md`.
- **The two tracks can run in parallel and can both end at the same Tribunal, by different doors.** A council's Professional Conduct Committee (PCC) can independently investigate and lay a charge before the Health Practitioners Disciplinary Tribunal (HPDT) - see `mcnz.md`. Separately, HDC's own complaint process, in the small minority of cases that proceed to a **formal investigation** and a **finding of breach**, can - per `hdc.org.nz`, "very rarely" - refer the matter to HDC's **Director of Proceedings**, whose role (per the site, though described there only briefly) is to consider taking legal action, which can include laying a charge before the same HPDT. So a practitioner can in principle face an HPDT charge that originated via a council's PCC, via HDC's Director of Proceedings, or (in theory) both - these are two distinct statutory pathways into the same disciplinary forum, not one process with two names.
- **Most complaints never reach that point.** HDC's own process description states a formal investigation happens in "only a small number of cases" - the four listed dispositions on receiving a complaint are: ask the provider to resolve it directly (sometimes with independent Advocacy Service help), refer to another agency, close the complaint if the care was reasonable, or (rarely) formally investigate.

## Investigation/complaint process

Per `hdc.org.nz/making-a-complaint/complaint-process/`, fetched live this session:

1. A complaint is filed with HDC (often after first raising the concern directly with the provider, which the site suggests can resolve things faster).
2. HDC's Complaints Assessment Team evaluates it.
3. The Commissioner (or a delegate) - the site's own description uses the phrase "like a judge" - chooses among: provider-led resolution (with optional Advocacy Service support), referral to another agency (e.g. a professional council), closure where care was reasonable, or formal investigation (the rare path).
4. Assessment methods can include requesting the provider's response, gathering information from other organisations, and seeking clinical advice from a peer in the same field as the provider.
5. The Commissioner issues a decision with an explanation to both parties, which can include recommendations for the provider.

## Sanction range

HDC does not itself impose the kind of practitioner-facing sanctions (suspension, cancellation of registration, fines) that the HPDT can under HPCAA s101 - see `mcnz.md`. Its own outputs are findings (breach / no breach of the Code), recommendations to providers, and, rarely, referral to its Director of Proceedings for possible legal proceedings (which can include an HPDT charge, or in principle other legal action) rather than a sanction HDC hands down directly. `UNVERIFIED`: the exact legal powers and scope of the Director of Proceedings role beyond what `hdc.org.nz` states in passing - the site's own description of this role was thin, and a dedicated page on it was not successfully located this session (a search for `hdc.org.nz`'s "role of the Commissioner" page and a Director of Proceedings-specific page did not resolve to a fetchable primary page this session).
