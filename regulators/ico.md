# ICO

The second regulator profile in this repository, following `cnil.md`'s format - see `README.md`.

## Statutory basis

The ICO is the UK's independent regulator for data protection and information rights, under the Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR, and separately regulates freedom-of-information compliance for public bodies under the Freedom of Information Act 2000. This profile covers its data-protection enforcement role; its FOI role has a different procedure and is not covered here.

## Investigation stages

Per `ico.org.uk` (checked 2026-08-22): an investigation runs from case opening and information gathering through to a decision on whether to exercise statutory enforcement powers. The ICO's corrective toolkit is broader than fines alone:

- **Information notices** - compel an organisation to provide information.
- **Assessment notices** - compel an organisation to permit an on-site assessment.
- **Enforcement notices** - require an organisation to take (or stop taking) specific action.
- **Penalty notices** - impose a financial penalty.

The ICO published a draft consultation on new enforcement procedural guidance in late 2025 (`ico.org.uk/media2/jakfp5aw/enforcement-prod-guidance-consultation-20251031.pdf`) - flagged `UNVERIFIED` whether this has been finalised and adopted as of any specific matter date; check the current version before relying on procedural specifics beyond the headline structure above.

## Sanction range

Per the sources checked: fines up to **£17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher** - the UK GDPR's own cap, set independently of the EU GDPR's €20 million/4% figure following Brexit. Do not assume the EU figure applies to a UK matter, or vice versa - they are separate caps under separate instruments even though structurally similar.

## Appeal route and forum

Per `gov.uk/guidance/information-rights-appeal-against-the-commissioners-decision` and reporting on the TikTok appeal (checked 2026-08-22): an organisation appeals a penalty notice to the **First-tier Tribunal (General Regulatory Chamber)** within **28 calendar days** of the decision. Unlike CNIL's appeal to the Conseil d'État (a public-law forum reviewing legality), the First-tier Tribunal **reviews the ICO's decision on the merits**, not only on procedural/legality grounds, and may cancel or reduce a penalty - a materially different standard of review from the French route, not a like-for-like equivalent.
