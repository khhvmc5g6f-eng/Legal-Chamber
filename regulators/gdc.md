# GDC

The eighth regulator profile in this repository - see `README.md`.

## Statutory basis

The GDC is the UK-wide statutory regulator of dentists and dental care professionals, under the **Dentists Act 1984** (as amended). It maintains the register and sets standards. Adjudication is carried out by its own in-house **Practice Committees** - the **Professional Conduct Committee (PCC)**, **Professional Performance Committee**, and **Health Committee** - plus a separate **Interim Orders Committee** and **Registration Appeals Committee**. This is the same in-house-committee model as the NMC and GPhC, not the arm's-length branded-tribunal-service model used by the GMC (MPTS) and HCPC (HCPTS).

## Investigation stages

Per `gdc-uk.org` (checked 2026-08-22): the GDC's own published guidance describes **Practice Committee proceedings** as having four stages: **(1) a preliminary stage**, **(2) factual inquiry**, **(3) submissions by the parties**, and **(4) the determination**. Ahead of a Practice Committee referral, the GDC's own performance reporting describes a funnel: concerns received are assessed, a subset proceed to **case examiners**, and a smaller subset are referred on to a **Practice Committee hearing** (the GDC's own 2025 figures, per the indexed snippet: of every 100 concerns received, on average 81 progressed to assessment, 34 to case examiners, and 15 to a Practice Committee hearing). The **Professional Conduct Committee** specifically decides whether an allegation amounts to misconduct and, if so, whether that misconduct means the registrant's fitness to practise is currently impaired.

## Sanction range

Per the sources checked, where impairment is found, in ascending order: **no further action**; a **reprimand** (a formal statement of disapproval; the practitioner remains free to practise unrestricted); **conditions of practice** (restrictions for a set period, which may include supervision, training, or evidencing improvement); **suspension** (a set period during which the registrant cannot practise, reviewed before expiry); and **erasure** from the register (the most serious sanction, reserved for dishonesty, serious misconduct, or conduct fundamentally incompatible with continued registration). The committee must act proportionately and impose the least severe sanction that protects the public and maintains confidence in the profession.

## Appeal route and forum

Per secondary legal-sector sources summarising the Dentists Act 1984 as currently amended (checked 2026-08-22): the appeal route was historically to the Privy Council under the original section 29, but the Act was later amended so that appeals against a Practice Committee determination or direction now lie to the **relevant court** - the **High Court of Justice in England and Wales**, the **Court of Session** (Scotland), or the **High Court of Justice in Northern Ireland**, depending on the registrant's address - the same tripartite structure as the GMC and HCPC. The time limit is **28 days** from notification. On appeal, the court can dismiss the appeal, allow it and quash the decision, substitute any other decision the Practice Committee could have made, or remit the case to the Practice Committee with directions.
