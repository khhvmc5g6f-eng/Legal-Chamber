# HIW

The twelfth regulator profile in this repository - see `README.md`. Like CQC and HIS, HIW is a **provider/service quality regulator**, not an individual-practitioner fitness-to-practise body.

## Statutory basis - and a materially different model from CQC, confirmed this session

HIW's own published material draws an explicit distinction (checked 2026-08-22, `hiw.org.uk`): it **inspects** NHS services in Wales, but **regulates** independent healthcare providers - two different words for two different legal relationships, not interchangeable. The **registration** power - under the **Care Standards Act 2000** and the **Independent Health Care (Wales) Regulations 2011** - applies to independent clinics and hospitals; operating an in-scope independent healthcare service without registration is a criminal offence under **section 11** of the 2000 Act. NHS Wales bodies are not required to register with HIW the way an NHS trust must register with CQC in England; HIW inspects them under separate powers instead. This mirrors the same registration/inspection split found in Scotland's HIS model (see `his.md`) - confirmed independently for both regulators this session, and both differ from CQC's single unified NHS-and-independent registration regime in England.

## Enforcement stages (independent healthcare registration track)

Per `hiw.org.uk` (checked 2026-08-22), for the independent-healthcare registration function specifically:

- **Registration** - a provider whose service is in scope under the Care Standards Act 2000 / Independent Health Care (Wales) Regulations 2011 must register before operating.
- **Requirement to cease provision** - where a service operates without registration, HIW's minimum response is to require it to stop until registration is achieved.
- **Prosecution** - unregistered operation can lead to prosecution, with conviction carrying a fine and/or imprisonment (specific maximum figures not independently confirmed this session - flagged below).
- **Notice of Proposal → Notice of Decision** - for registered providers facing enforcement action (e.g. condition changes or cancellation), HIW issues a Notice of Proposal; the provider can make written representations; if HIW is not persuaded and confirms the action via a Notice of Decision, the provider has a right to appeal (see below).

## Sanction range

For the independent-healthcare registration track: a **requirement to cease** unregistered provision, **conditions** on registration, **cancellation of registration**, and **criminal prosecution** (fine and/or imprisonment) for operating without registration under section 11 of the Care Standards Act 2000. No specific fine ceiling or maximum custodial term was independently confirmed against the Act's own penalty provision this session - flagged `UNVERIFIED` below.

## Appeal route and forum

Per `legislation.gov.uk`'s text of the Care Standards Act 2000 and corroborating HIW/gov.uk sources (checked 2026-08-22): under **section 21** of the Act, a decision by HIW in the exercise of its registration functions can be appealed to the **First-tier Tribunal (Care Standards)** - the same UK-wide tribunal, and the same **28-day** time limit from service of the decision, used by CQC in England (see `cqc.md`). This is a materially different forum from HIS's Scotland route, which goes to the sheriff on a 14-day window (see `his.md`) - despite HIW and HIS both regulating on the same independent-healthcare-registration/NHS-inspection split, their appeal mechanics diverge, following each jurisdiction's own court/tribunal system.
