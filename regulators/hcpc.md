# HCPC

The sixth regulator profile in this repository - see `README.md`.

## Statutory basis

The HCPC is the UK-wide statutory regulator of a defined list of allied health and associated professions - including paramedics, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, radiographers, operating department practitioners, biomedical scientists, and (in England only, since 2012) social workers - under the **Health and Social Work Professions Order 2001** (a statutory instrument; also referred to in older material as the Health Professions Order 2001, its name before social work regulation was added). Adjudication is carried out by the **Health and Care Professions Tribunal Service (HCPTS)**, which the HCPC's own material describes as having "a distinct identity" from the HCPC to emphasise that hearings are run by independent panels at arm's length - structurally similar to the GMC/MPTS split, though the HCPTS (unlike the MPTS) is not a separate statutory committee created by its own Act provision in the same way.

## Investigation stages

Per `hcpc-uk.org` (checked 2026-08-22): the process runs through:

- **Triage** - an initial assessment of whether a concern falls within the HCPC's remit.
- **Investigation** - where a concern is in scope, the HCPC investigates and drafts allegations for the registrant to respond to.
- **Case preparation** - the case moves to a Case Preparation and Conclusion team, which instructs external legal counsel to complete the investigation and prepare for a final hearing.
- **Preliminary hearings** - deal with procedural, evidential, timetabling, or case-management issues ahead of a substantive hearing.
- **Final (substantive) hearings** - a panel determines whether the allegation is "well-founded": whether the facts are proved, whether they amount to the stated statutory ground, and whether fitness to practise is currently impaired.

## Sanction range

Per the sources checked, the HCPC/HCPTS can: refer a case for **mediation** (available in the legislation but not itself classed as a sanction); take **no action**; impose a **caution order** (1-5 years); impose a **conditions of practice order** (up to 3 years); impose a **suspension order** (up to 12 months, the maximum single period though it can be extended on review); or **strike the registrant off** the register (the most serious sanction - reserved for fundamental dishonesty, serious sexual misconduct, conduct fundamentally incompatible with continued registration, or no realistic prospect of remediation). Panels are directed to start from the least restrictive sanction and work upward only where necessary, giving reasons for rejecting each lesser option.

## Appeal route and forum

Per `hcpc-uk.org`'s published consolidated Health and Social Work Professions Order 2001 and corroborating secondary sources (checked 2026-08-22): under **Article 38** of the Order, a final decision of the Conduct and Competence Committee or Health Committee can be appealed to the **High Court** (England & Wales), the **Court of Session** (Scotland), or the **High Court of Justice in Northern Ireland** - the same tripartite UK venue structure seen in the GMC and GDC regimes. The time limit is **28 days** from the date of service of the decision (Article 29(10)), and extensions are granted only in exceptional circumstances.
