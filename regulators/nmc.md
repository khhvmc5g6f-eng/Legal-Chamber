# NMC

The fifth regulator profile in this repository - see `README.md`.

## Statutory basis

The NMC is the UK-wide statutory regulator of nurses, midwives, and nursing associates, under the **Nursing and Midwifery Order 2001** (a statutory instrument, not an Act). It keeps the register and sets standards of proficiency and conduct (`The Code`). Unlike the GMC/MPTS split, the NMC runs its own **Fitness to Practise Committee (FtPC)** in-house rather than delegating adjudication to a separately branded arm's-length tribunal service - this is a structurally different model from the GMC's, not a like-for-like equivalent.

## Investigation stages

Per `nmc.org.uk` (fitness-to-practise library, fetched 2026-08-22) and corroborating secondary sources: the process runs through:

- **Initial screening** - the NMC receives a concern and identifies the regulatory issue it raises.
- **Investigation** - the NMC gathers information: statements, expert opinion, and clinical advice relevant to the allegation.
- **Case examiner decision** - case examiners (a mix of registrants and lay people) decide, at the close of investigation, whether there is a case to answer. They can close the case, give advice, issue a warning, or agree undertakings without a full hearing, or refer the case onward.
- **Fitness to Practise Committee hearing** - if referred, an FtPC panel holds a hearing, generally in public. The panel first decides whether the facts found proved amount to misconduct (or lack of competence, or a health-related impairment), then whether fitness to practise is currently impaired, then - if so - what sanction to impose.

## Sanction range

Per the sources checked, in ascending order: **no further action**; a **caution order** (lasting 1-5 years, no restriction on practice); a **conditions of practice order** (restricting practice for up to 3 years, reviewed before expiry); a **suspension order** (up to 12 months, reviewed before expiry - used for serious patient-care failures, significant medication errors causing harm, serious boundary violations, dishonesty short of the striking-off threshold, or health concerns conditions can't manage); and a **striking-off order** (permanent removal - the most serious sanction; a struck-off registrant cannot apply for restoration for 5 years).

## Appeal route and forum

Per `nmc.org.uk`'s published Nursing and Midwifery Order 2001 text and corroborating secondary sources (checked 2026-08-22): appeals against fitness-to-practise decisions are made under the Order - a statutory appeal to the **High Court** (England & Wales), presumably the equivalent courts in Scotland and Northern Ireland by the same UK-wide pattern seen in the GMC/HCPC/GDC regimes (this parallel is inferred from the shared statutory drafting family, not independently confirmed for the NMC specifically this session - flagged below). The time limit is **28 days** from the date of the decision (Article 29(10) of the Order). Separately, Article 30(7) provides an internal review mechanism: where new evidence relevant to a striking-off order becomes available after the order is made, the FtPC may review it under a process that mirrors a restoration application.
