# Medical Board of Australia

The fifth regulator profile in this repository, following `ico.md`'s format — see `README.md`. This is the doctor-specific companion to `ahpra.md`: read the AHPRA/National Board relationship section there first, since the Medical Board of Australia (MBA) is one specific instance of that general structure — the National Board for the medical profession, one of fifteen National Boards operating under AHPRA's administrative umbrella.

## Statutory basis

The MBA is established under the **Health Practitioner Regulation National Law**, the same template law that establishes AHPRA and the other fourteen National Boards. It is the profession-specific decision-making body for doctors under the National Registration and Accreditation Scheme (NRAS) — see `ahpra.md` for the scheme-wide structure.

## Role and functions

Per corroborating search results checked live 2026-08-22 (medicalboard.gov.au's own "About" page returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch; this is built from third-party professional/regulatory summaries describing the MBA's own published role, not from training-data recall):

- **Registration**: registers medical practitioners and medical students across Australia — general, specialist, provisional, limited, and non-practising registration categories, plus student registration.
- **Standard-setting**: develops registration standards, codes, and guidelines for the medical profession (e.g. continuing professional development requirements, the cosmetic-surgery-specific standards described in `ahpra.md`'s reforms section).
- **Notifications and discipline**: is the statutory decision-maker on notifications (complaints) about doctors — assessment, investigation oversight, panel hearings, and referral of the most serious matters to tribunal — per the general process described in `ahpra.md`. AHPRA administers this process on the Board's behalf; the Board makes the decisions.
- **International qualifications**: oversees assessment of overseas-trained doctors seeking to practise in Australia.
- **Accreditation**: approves accreditation standards and accredited courses of medical study (working with the Australian Medical Council, AMC, as the accrediting body).

## Investigation stages, sanction range, and appeal route

These are **not duplicated here** — they are the same National Law process, sanctions (caution/reprimand, conditions, undertakings, tribunal-only suspension/cancellation/fines), and appeal route (external appeal to the relevant state/territory civil and administrative tribunal, e.g. NCAT in NSW, within 28 days) described in detail in `ahpra.md`, since that process is common to all fifteen National Boards under the one National Law. See `ahpra.md` for the full description and its sourcing caveats (including the UNVERIFIED flags on the specific fine figure and on Tasmania/WA's tribunal).
