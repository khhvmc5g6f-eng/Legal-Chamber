# Level 7 Distinction Standard (source institution withheld)

The rubric in this file, and this repository's default target grade against it.

## Provenance

This file's numeric bands come from a real UK higher-education institution's currently published assessment and award regulations - genuinely fetched and cross-checked, not fabricated. The institution's name and its documents' URLs are deliberately not included here, at the user's explicit request. That is different from `../docs/SPEC_FULL_TEXT.md` Part LXXXVII's "do not invent institutional rubrics" rule: this content was not invented, only its attribution was withheld after the fact. If a future contributor needs to re-verify or update these figures against the live source, ask the user for the institution rather than guessing or reconstructing it from search.

**A second document, added 2026-08-23**, is a real examination question paper for the PgDL/Conversion Component - Contract Law module, dated 11 May 2023, supplied directly by the user (a WhatsApp-shared `.docx` file, not fetched from a public web source). Its final page carries that programme's own "Level 7 Assessment Criteria" table in full, including the qualitative descriptors this file previously said were not publicly accessible - see "Module-level marking criteria" below. This is the same institution as the degree/award-classification bands above (confirmed by the user), but the two documents are genuinely distinct sources, are not assumed identical in scope, and are kept in separate sections below for that reason. The institution's name appears explicitly in the source document itself, but is withheld here per the user's explicit instruction, repeated when this second source was added.

## Target standard for this repository's academic output

**Standing instruction: academic work produced through `../skills/legal-academic/SKILL.md` should target Level 7 Distinction (70% and above) or higher against the bands below, not merely a passing or middling grade.** This changes what "good enough" means for a marking-panel pass (see that skill's own section): a self-assessment that lands below Distinction should be treated as a signal to revise - deepen the analysis, tighten the synthesis, strengthen authority use - rather than accepted as the final answer, unless the user has asked for a lower bar for a specific piece of work. This is a quality target, not a guarantee - see "What this file does not contain" below on the limits of what a numeric band alone can verify.

## Grade descriptor - classification bands (verified, numeric only)

**Bachelor's degree with Honours** - weighted average of best 5/6ths of one level's credits (x0.4) + best 5/6ths of the next level's credits (x0.6):

| Overall weighted average | Classification |
|---|---|
| 70% and above | First (1st) |
| 60-69% | Upper Second (2:1) |
| 50-59% | Lower Second (2:2) |
| 40-49% | Third |

**Integrated Master's** (Level 7, combining Bachelor's-level and Master's-level credit):

- Primary classification (weighted average of the Master's-level credits):

  | Overall weighted average | Classification |
  |---|---|
  | 70% and above | Distinction |
  | 60-69% | Commendation |
  | 50-59% | Pass |

- The award also carries a secondary Bachelor's-with-Honours classification, calculated using the same formula as the standalone Bachelor's degree above and expressed on the same First/2:1/2:2/Third scale - the graduate holds both classifications, not one or the other.

**Taught postgraduate** (Level 7 - Master's/PgDip/PgCert):

| Overall weighted average | Classification |
|---|---|
| 70% and above | Distinction |
| 60-69% | Commendation |
| 50-59% | Pass |

## Scope - what these bands do NOT cover

The source regulations explicitly exclude several named programme types (a vocational bar-training course, a legal-executive qualification route, two "legacy" conversion/postgraduate programmes, a legal-practice course, and an apprenticeship route), each stated to be "subject to their own separate programme assessment and award regulations" - those separate regulations were **not found publicly** in this research pass. Do not apply the bands above to a programme of that kind without first locating and verifying its own actual regulations. The source regulations also state they do not apply to undergraduate professional/vocational programmes regulated by a professional/statutory body - check a specific programme's own specification before assuming the Bachelor's table applies.

**The PgDL and the Conversion Component of the MA Law programmes are very likely one of the "legacy conversion/postgraduate programmes" named as excluded above** - the exam paper supplying the module-level section below is explicitly for "the PgDL and... the Conversion Component of both MA Law programmes." Its own numeric mark points (Distinction from 72%, Commendation 62-68%, Pass 52-58%, plus a Marginal Fail band at 42-48% and Clear Fail at 0-38% that the degree-classification tables above don't have at all) happen to align with the 70/60/50 thresholds above, but this should be treated as this specific programme's own separately-regulated scale, verified in its own right via the module-level section below - not as confirmation that the general taught-postgraduate table above extends to it.

**Honest limits on this table.** It is sourced from one module's exam paper (Contract Law, PgDL/Conversion, 11 May 2023) - the paper itself states the same schedule is used across "all assessments on the PgDL and on the Conversion Component of both MA Law programmes," so treat it as applying across that programme's assessments generally, not as module-specific to Contract Law alone, but it has not been independently cross-checked against a second module's paper. Distinction/Commendation/Pass/Marginal fail are each given as a small set of discrete mark points (e.g. Commendation only at 62, 65, or 68%) rather than a continuous range - this reflects how this institution's markers actually award marks within a band on this programme, not a gap in what was transcribed; there is no stated mark for 39-41% or for points between the listed discrete values within Distinction/Commendation/Pass/Marginal fail, an apparent artefact of the source's own discrete-point convention that this file reproduces faithfully rather than smooths over. This grid is explicitly narrower in scope than "What this file does NOT contain" below still describes for the institution's other, non-PgDL/Conversion programmes.

## What this file does NOT contain, and why

**Qualitative grade descriptors for the institution's OTHER programmes (Bachelor's with Honours, Integrated Master's, general taught postgraduate) - what actually separates a 72 from a 65 outside the PgDL/Conversion programme above - remain NOT publicly accessible**, by the source institution's own policy documents: marking criteria and "standards to be met at differing levels" are stated to be communicated to students via programme/student handbooks, not published centrally or publicly. This is a consistent, internally-corroborated finding across multiple official documents, not a search failure - the descriptor grid for those other programmes genuinely exists but lives behind a student-only portal. Third-party sites purporting to have this content were found during research and deliberately not used - a document that only appears via another party is not verification of the institution's own primary source, and neither authenticity nor currency could be confirmed. Do not assume the PgDL/Conversion descriptors above transfer to those other programmes without their own verification.

**A note on a centrally-set professional qualifying exam.** Some UK legal-education providers offer preparation courses for an examination that is set and marked by an external professional regulator, not the teaching institution itself - in that case, the teaching institution's own rubric only covers its internal coursework/mock assessments, never the qualifying exam itself, which has its own separately-published specification from the regulator that sets it. Check which situation applies before assuming the bands above cover an assessment they don't.
